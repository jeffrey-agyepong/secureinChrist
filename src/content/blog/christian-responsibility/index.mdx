In life one thing is certain: failure, “for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God” and “Do not bring Your servant into judgment, for no one alive is righteous in your sight” (Romans 3:23 & Psalm 143:2).

Although we have victory over sin (John 3:16), the Christian life is not the perfect life, it is still a life at war with sin (1 John 8). So the question is not how can I avoid ever making a mistake in life, the question is how do I take responsibility when I make a mistake? We recently witnessed a high profile case of this on the internet, with the plagiarism of popular apologist Wes Huff. Initially, when a skeptic of Christianity uncovered Huff for plagiarism, Huff did not concede to committing the academic fraud and theft. Instead what we saw was other online Christian figures, such as Mike Winger, come to call for accountability. The author of this article was intending to write commending Winger and others for keeping in line with Christian integrity, sadly excluding Huff from it. That was until Huff himself offered a full apology. Some may speculate that Huff used AI to assist in writing it, but either way it is an apology.

In 2 Samuel 12, we see King David fail spectacularly. We see God confront David through Nathan. Although Huff’s sin is not nearly as grievous as David’s, we can draw some comparisons. For example, David did not go beyond the reach of God’s mercy, we can recognise that Huff is still a Christian. 

\
The Christian life is not a perfect life, it is one that still wars with sin and relies on God’s mercy. The question is not how can I avoid ever making a mistake, although we should endeavour to try, but the question is how do I take responsibility for when I do? It’s moments like these where we become mindful of our own failures too. We want to commend Wes Huff for taking responsibility and owning up to plagiarism.
