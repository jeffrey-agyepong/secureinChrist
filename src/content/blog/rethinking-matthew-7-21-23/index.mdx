### *Matthew 7:21-23 (Christian Standard Bible)*

> 21 “Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but only the one who does the will of my Father in heaven. 22 On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, didn’t we prophesy in your name, drive out demons in your name, and do many miracles in your name?’ 23 Then I will announce to them, ‘I never knew you. Depart from me, you lawbreakers!’\[a]\[b]

\
Growing up in church, this passage of Scripture always frightened me. I did not like that it was included in the pages of Scripture to be quite frank. The idea that I could live my entire life thinking I was a true believer, only for Jesus to tell me “I never knew you”. For several years, passages like this left me in a pit of despair that I couldn’t pull myself out of. What’s the point of this Christian walk, if there’s a possibility it could all be in vain? But what if I told you, you’ve probably misunderstood this passage and that Jesus was not trying to stir up fear in believers but rather call out the self-righteous.

\
A key principle in understanding Scripture is ‘Scripture interprets Scripture’ and this principle is vital in understanding this passage.

\
To properly understand this passage a couple questions have to be considered:

1\. What did they believe was their justification for entry into heaven?

2\. What is the ‘will of my Father’?

3\. Why did Jesus call them lawbreakers?

### 1. What did they believe was their justification for entry into heaven?

The professors of faith point to their **works** (Romans 3:20) as their basis of justification instead of the *finished work of Christ of the cross* ([John 19:30](./Bible.html)), for the full payment of their sin that is freely(Isaiah 55:1) offered to all (John 3:16). Salvation is by faith alone through grace alone in Christ alone apart from works (Ephesians 2:8-9). No one will ever enter heaven based on their own good works (Isaiah 64:6).
